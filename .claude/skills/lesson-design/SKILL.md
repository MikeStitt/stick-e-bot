# Constitution — Lesson design part

Read this when writing or revising a session plan, a rubric, or the course arc. Read with the
[constitution](../../rules/constitution.md) and [`onshape`](../onshape/SKILL.md).

## Checkpoints

A checkpoint is a **visible result**, not a line on a checklist. The test is whether the instructor
can walk the room and see at a glance who is there and who is not. "Student has extruded the block"
is a checkpoint; "student understands extrusion" is not.

Every session ends with a **published, named Onshape version** representing the floor. That version
is the next session's starting point and the recovery path for anyone absent or behind (Working
Rule 13).

## Vocabulary

- Introduce terms deliberately and reuse them. A term used once is not taught.
- Use Onshape's own words for Onshape's own things — see [`onshape`](../onshape/SKILL.md).
  Inventing a friendlier synonym for a tool means the student cannot find it in the menu.
- Do not use a term in session 3 that was never introduced in sessions 1–2. Read the neighbors
  before you write (Working Rule 4).

## Anticipating failure

Every block names the mistakes it will provoke and the recovery for each. These are known in
advance — under-defined sketches, extrudes going the wrong way, a lost view orientation, a student
who deleted a feature and does not know undo exists. Writing the recovery down once beats the mentor
improvising it thirty times.

## Assessment

- **Rubrics describe observable results**, not inferred understanding: "sketch is fully defined
  without assistance" — not "student grasps constraints."
- **Grade against the floor.** The ceiling is opportunity, not a scoring band; scoring it penalizes
  the students the floor exists for.
- One rubric per session, in that session's folder, written at the same time as the plan it grades.
