When I do not know how Onshape behaves, read `.docs/onshape-gui-howto.md` first. It is the repo's
own record of driving the UI, written from earlier runs, and it is far more specific than anything
online: toolbar geometry, what each dialog does, what Slot actually does. After that, take the
user's account (Mike), because he CADs in this tool daily, or read Onshape's own documentation.
Proposing to open the CAD and test every dialog, every feature type, or every menu to establish a
general fact is not a valid plan and I should not offer it.

**Why:** the user (Mike) reporting his own practice is evidence, and treating it as something to be
independently re-derived is both slow and a little insulting. Exhaustive enumeration also does not
scale — there are dozens of feature dialogs — so the plan cannot finish and the question stays
open while the run stalls.

On 2026-08-28 three attempts went into clicking a slot out of empty space; the how-to already said
the tool wraps a curve the sketch holds, and where the diameter label lands.

**How to apply:** grep the how-to, then ask him, then look it up. Reserve driving the CAD for
facts about *this model* — a dimension, a face, a state — which is what
[[onshape-bodydetails-beats-featurescript]] and the "measurements conclude" rule are for. The
constitution's "never claim a verification you did not perform" governs what I may assert, not
where the evidence has to come from: "the user (Mike) says" is a citable source, and so is Onshape's
documentation.
