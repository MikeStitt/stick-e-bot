  "Never coin a term or a gate that the contract does not define; say the concern in plain words
  instead."

I invented the phrase "signed off" in draft9p1p5's register, carried it into draft9p1p6's and into
draft9p4's plan, and built a blocking decision out of it. No document defines it. The work it
was supposedly waiting on had already been done: the user
(Mike) printed draft9p1p5 and draft9p1p6 and said he liked both.

**Why:** a coined term reads as a real requirement to anyone downstream, so it manufactures work and
hides what is actually owed. And the user (Mike) is the reader; a concern he cannot parse is not a
concern he has been told.

**How to apply:** when raising a concern, name the evidence that is missing and who can supply it,
in words the contract already uses. If a result arrives that closes an open item, correct the record
that carries it the same day, including a published register. See
[[instructions-state-facts-not-importance]] and [[one-fact-one-home]].
