Where a plain English word carries the same meaning as a technical one, use the plain word.
The established example: **"handedness", not "chirality"** — for a part that is or is not
its own mirror image.

**Why:** the audience is middle-schoolers who are smart but new to CAD. A technical synonym
buys nothing and costs a reader. This is not the same as talking down to them — the concept
stays whole, only the label changes.

**How to apply:** applies to the working notes in `.docs/` as well as the student-facing
guide, because the notes feed the guide and the word travels. Genuine CAD vocabulary the
students must learn (coincident, revolve, merge scope, clevis, blade) stays — that is the
subject, not decoration. See [[drawing-conventions]] for the parallel rule about what
belongs on a drawing.
