Write instructions as advice to someone who is going to succeed. Say what to do and what
you will see when it works. Do not write the failure — not as a warning, not as "watch out
for", not as a parenthetical. The information is the same either way; only the framing
changes, and students read the framing.

Concretely: admonitions are **advice**, not warnings. "Press `n` once and the cube in the
corner reads Front — that's you looking straight at the plane" carries the same fact as
"press `n` twice and you are silently mirrored", and only one of them assumes the reader
is about to fail.

**Why:** the first `robot-guide` and the first `robot-guide2` spike were both written as a
list of things that bite you. The user read that as talking down to students and as a text
biased toward expecting them to fail — "they understand what you are implying."

**How to apply:** when a build finding is worth teaching, convert it before it reaches the
page. Find the action that produces the good outcome and the observation that confirms it,
and write those two. Keep the failure in the run's build notes, where the audience is the
next builder, not the student. See [[plain-words-for-middle-schoolers]] and
[[instructions-state-facts-not-importance]].
