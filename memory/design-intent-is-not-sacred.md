Never rank the design sources against each other — sketches, briefs, and whether the thing
actually assembles and articulates all sit at one level. When a requirement is costing
disproportionate work, treat the requirement as a candidate for change, not just the work.

**Why:** ranking makes one source unchangeable, and an unchangeable source turns every conflict
into work squeezed around it. The reference robot's "150 mm tall" is the worked example: while it
was a requirement the design bent to reach it, and once the parts were allowed to fall where the
joints put them the problem got smaller and the answer got honest. How much work a constraint is
causing is evidence about the constraint.

**How to apply:** when a conflict appears, say what it would cost to change each side, including
the requirement itself — do not silently treat the stated number as fixed and optimize around it.
Present changing the requirement as a real option with its price. Related: the user still decides
— see [[instructions-state-facts-not-importance]] and [[cad-models-need-design-intent]], which is
about a model surviving a driving-dimension change, not about which dimension is allowed to drive.
