Hip joint · section on the ball center · Ø6 stalk, 10 mm to the torso’s underside

The hip reaches its full travel without touching the torso.

The thigh swings until the stalk meets the socket’s mouth, at 41.76° either way. Nothing outside the socket gets in the way: at that limit the collar’s rim is still 2.554 mm below the torso. The travel is set by the joint’s own internals, which is what the design intends. No pedestal, no longer stalk, no change.

±41.76°both axes, unobstructed

What limits it

Two things touch inside the socket, and neither is the torso. The stalk runs into the mouth’s edge, and how soon it gets there is set by #grip — the 1.9465 mm the ball center sits inside the collar’s face. The mouth is held at 96% of the ball whatever the fit is, so retention is fixed and the swing is what a tighter fit buys: at #fit 0.8 this drawing showed 28.0°.

The 2.554 is air, not interference

The stud belongs to the torso and the collar belongs to the thigh. They are printed as separate objects and snapped together, so the gap between them is not a feature any slicer ever sees. At a 0.4 mm nozzle it is 6.4 extrusion widths of nothing.

It is recorded here only as the proof that the collar clears — not as a number to improve.

The arithmetic

θmax = arccos(stalkR / cavR) − arcsin(grip / cavR) = arccos(3 / 6.08) − arcsin(1.94648 / 6.08) = 60.434° − 18.672° = 41.763° rim high = 1.94648·cosθ + 9·sinθ = 7.446 rim out = 9·cosθ − 1.94648·sinθ = 5.417 air = 10 − 7.446 = 2.554
