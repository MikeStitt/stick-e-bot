Socket study — what I'm looking at

Dated 2026-08-11, and kept that way. These are renders of a model that no longer exists, with a Ø16 thigh and #grip 1.5, and one of the notes on them — that the head’s face would block the swing — turned out to be wrong. The robot has since doubled: the limb is Ø24, the ball Ø12, #grip 1.9465 and the swing 41.76°. For the numbers to build to, read the current plan sheets and the build briefs.

Four server-side renders of socket-in-context, with the things I flagged marked up. Ball Ø6, clearance 0.2, grip 1.5, collar Ø9.4 × 5.5 long, wall 1.5, four 0.8 mm slits, on a Ø16 thigh.

1 — The section is where the problems are

The sharp mouth rim

missing

The top edge of the collar is a square break straight into the spherical cavity. There is no lead-in chamfer, so the ball has to force a sharp corner apart on the way in. This is the single feature that decides how the joint feels, and it is the one I said mattered most before building neither of the two angles that set it.

The slit root

missing

Each slit runs the full 5.5 mm of the collar and then stops dead at the shoulder, in a sharp internal corner — exactly where tab bending strain peaks. It wants a relief groove with a filleted root, which both moves the stress riser off the corner and sets the free length deliberately instead of by accident.

2 — The plan view, which I misread first time

Everything here is correct

confirmed

Reading outward from the centre: the dark sphere is the cavity seen through the mouth; the four petals are the tabs; the crossed gaps are the 0.8 mm slits; the arc bounding the petals is the collar at Ø9.4; the flat ring outside it is the thigh's own top face.

That flat ring is the step-back — 3.3 mm per side — and it is the whole reason a boss buys anything. Without it the ball's stalk binds on the parent's face instead of swinging.

3 — A correction to something I told you

I said the head's flat face would block the ball's swing. That was wrong.

Working it properly: the stalk is Ø3 passing through a Ø5.653 mouth, so it has 1.33 mm of clearance each side at a plane 1.5 mm above the ball centre. It binds on the mouth rim at roughly 40°.

The parent face sits 5.5 mm below that rim and well outside the swing. The collar lifts the joint clear on the block exactly as it does on the rod. Right principle, wrong geometry.

4 — Mine against the validated build

The grey block is the validation agent's actual part, linked to its published version. It sits on the head because Derived places at base origin — cosmetic, and I can move it.

  • Grey — GUI built, cavity derived from the ball by Subtract‑with‑Offset, grip 2.0, no collar, no slits.
  • Blue — FeatureScript, cavity drawn as a Ø6.4 sphere, grip 1.5, collar and slits.

Both guarantee concentricity, by different means. But the grey one uses the method we actually validated, and mine does not — so this page is a shape reference, not the spec to hand an agent.

Verdict

The concept holds and the numbers check out. The socket is a self-contained fitting: it looks and behaves the same on a flat block and on a round rod, which means one feature can serve the head, the limbs, the hand and the foot.

It is not yet a joint. Three things stand between this and something worth printing: the lead-in chamfer, the slit root relief, and names on the parts — that last one breaking a rule I added to parts/onshape.md the same morning.
