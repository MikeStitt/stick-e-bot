The fork as slices of the limb

Dated 2026-08-11, and kept that way. It divides a Ø12 limb four ways and the sizing was later redone on section modulus over free length, which put the split at ear 3.2 and tab 2.1 rather than what is drawn here. The robot has since doubled: the limb is Ø24, the ball Ø12, #grip 1.9465 and the swing 41.76°. For the numbers to build to, read the current plan sheets and the build briefs.

Blade 3.0 mm, and the ears extended so each face is a full chord — so every layer fills the whole Ø12 section it sits in, and the ends follow the limb's own surface.

The corner constraint disappears

The old paddle was a 9.75 × 7.0 rectangle whose corners were the binding feature. Now nothing is cut off at an arbitrary width — each layer runs out to the arc.

Ear cross-section grows 13% (13.17 mm² against 11.70), and the blade's two corners now sit exactly on the Ø12 surface — the pink rings. Span drops from 7.0 to 6.0 because the blade went 4.0 → 3.0.

The real prize is the teeth

Round end goes from r 4.87 to r 5.81, so the tooth band moves out to r 4.60 → 5.50. Tooth width scales with radius, so everything gets easier at once:

One consequence to accept

A bigger round end sweeps a bigger circle, so the fork's bridge must move out with it: d ≥ 6.11 instead of 6.0. Sweep becomes 91.0° each way rather than exactly 90°.

Nothing is lost — it is still past the right angle — but the round number is gone unless the round end is trimmed back below the full chord.
