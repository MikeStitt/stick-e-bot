Wrap is not what holds the ball. The middle panel wraps the ball through exactly the same angle as the current CAD — it is a pure doubling, so every proportion is preserved — and it still drops the ball. The cavity grew by #fit and the ball did not, so the mouth opened to Ø12.482 against a Ø12 ball. Retention is the mouth against the ball, and nothing else.

Tightening #fit is what buys it back, and it does it without reaching further around the ball. The mouth is the dimension now, drawn at 96% of the ball — Ø11.520 against Ø12, so 0.480 mm of retention — and #grip is whatever puts the cavity sphere through a mouth that size: 1.9465 at #fit 0.08. That is 32% of the ball's radius against the current CAD's 45%. The socket wraps less and holds more.

What it buys

Swing rises from 37.1° to 41.8°. The stalk hits the rim of the mouth later because the rim reaches less far around the ball, and that is the whole of the trade in the other direction: every degree of wrap given back is a degree of articulation returned. Wrap goes from 116.7° to 108.9° and coverage from 72.5% to 66.2%, and the joint holds better than the one that was built and assembled.

The route not taken was to leave #fit at 0.8 and raise #grip to 3.60, which also closes the mouth. It wraps 126.9° and swings 31.9°, and it only holds near the fit it was dimensioned at — loosen the fit and the mouth opens past the ball again. Holding the mouth at 96% survives every value of #fit; the arithmetic is in .docs/experiments/runs/2026-08-25-draft9p1/a2-fit.md.

The collar is #ball + 2×#wall, which leaves the fit out on purpose so a printing clearance changes the hollow and nothing else — stickbot's built collar is Ø9.4 because it predates that rule. Drawn by .docs/experiments/sketches/socket-wrap.py from the four inputs of each sizing — ball, stalk, #fit and #grip. Every other number on this page is derived. Wrap is measured on the ball: the mouth plane sits #grip above the center, so the socket reaches asin(#grip / r) past the equator.
