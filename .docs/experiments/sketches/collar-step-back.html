The collar step-back — and why a bigger limb is worse

Dated 2026-08-11, and kept that way. The trade it shows is real — collar height buys swing, limb diameter spends it — but it puts the stalk binding at about 40°, and derived from the joint the limit was 37.09° even then. The robot has since doubled: the limb is Ø24, the ball Ø12, #grip 1.9465 and the swing 41.76°. For the numbers to build to, read the current plan sheets and the build briefs.

Section through a ball joint at the end of a limb. Solid = at rest; dashed = the limb swung until it collides. The ball, collar and stalk are identical in both panels; only the limb diameter changes.

What the drawing shows

The joint stops when the socket limb's top rim swings up and meets the face of the part the ball is rooted in. That rim sits at the limb's radius, so the wider the limb, the further out the rim is, and the sooner it arrives.

Ø12 swings 17° further than Ø16. The bigger step-back does not buy travel — it comes with a bigger rim, which costs travel.

What actually buys the travel

Compare the last two columns. Boring the socket straight into the limb's end face — no collar — gives 56° on Ø12. Standing the collar 5.5 mm proud lifts the joint centre clear of the rim and takes it to 78°.

It is the collar's height that buys the swing, not the radial gap around it. Height moves the pivot away from the rim; radial gap just makes the rim wider.

Where I had this wrong

I told you Ø16 was the one thing the ball socket liked, because its step-back grows from 1.3 to 3.3 mm. That is backwards. The step-back grows because the limb grew, and the limb growing is what costs the swing.

So the ball joint has no reason to prefer Ø16 either. With the hinge respecified to a 7.0 mm fork inside Ø12, nothing in the design now wants the larger limb.

The caveat worth testing

These numbers assume the mating part's face sits 5 mm above the ball centre and the socket limb's face 4 mm below it. Both are current plan values, and both are adjustable — raising the stand-off buys swing at both diameters.

The other limit, the stalk binding in the mouth at about 40°, is unaffected by any of this and is reached first. So in practice the mouth is the real constraint and these rim numbers are headroom — which is another reason not to have spent limb diameter on it.
